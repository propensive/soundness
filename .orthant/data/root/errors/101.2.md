# `↯/s.101.2` — `Socket.Error`

Raised when an established socket connection fails during use.

## Cause

This is distinct from a failure to connect, which is a `ConnectError`. A `Socket.Error`
means the connection was made and then failed — during an accept, a transmission, or a
close.

## Variants

### `↯/s.101.2.1` — `Accept`

**Example.** `[↯/s.101.2.1] a new connection could not be accepted`

### `↯/s.101.2.2` — `Transmit`

**Example.** `[↯/s.101.2.2] data could not be transmitted to the connection`

### `↯/s.101.2.3` — `Close`

**Example.** `[↯/s.101.2.3] the connection could not be closed cleanly`

### `↯/s.101.2.4` — `Handshake`

**Example.** `[↯/s.101.2.4] the TLS handshake with the peer failed`

### `↯/s.101.2.5` — `Timeout`

A datagram exchange (`Socket.Backend#exchangeUdp`) or a receive on a socket configured with
`Socket.Option.Timeout` waited the whole timeout without a datagram arriving.

**Example.** `[↯/s.101.2.5] no response arrived before the timeout`


## Resolution

The reason distinguishes the operation that failed. A connection failing during
transmission is usually the peer closing, a timeout, or the network; retrying is reasonable
where the protocol is idempotent.
